# dependent-optics

A Rust implementation of Pietro Vertechi, *Dependent Optics*, arXiv:2204.09547 (EPTCS 380, 2023, pp. 128–144). The PDF is the file `2204.09547` in this directory.

The crate is executable category theory on skeletal finite sets, plus the scalar and tensor lenses the paper uses for reverse-mode differentiation. Neural nets are those tensor lenses. Their forward and backward passes lower to NVIDIA kernels. The largest one compiled here is a Mixtral block: sliding-window grouped-query attention, rotary positions, and a top-2 mixture of SwiGLU experts.

```bash
cargo test --lib
```

There are no third-party Rust dependencies. Edition 2021. The exhaustive checks use carriers of cardinality at most [`MAX_CARD`](src/finset.rs) (`2`). Associativity of optics is exhaustive at cardinality 1; units and normalize/embed round-trips also run at cardinality 2. The NVIDIA test compiles with `nvcc` and runs on the GPU; see [Kernels](#kernels).

`compose(g, f)` always means `g ∘ f`: `f` is applied first.

## What a dependent optic is

Fix a bicategory `B` and two indexed categories `L, R: B^op → Cat` (Definition 1). An object is a triple `(X, X')_A`: a base object `A` and an object of each fibre. A morphism to `(Y, Y')_B` is an equivalence class of witnesses

```text
⟨l | r⟩    with representative 1-cell f: A → B,
           l: X → f* Y in L_A,
           r: f*' Y' → X' in R_A.
```

The equivalence is generated by a 2-cell `m: f ⇒ g`:

```text
(L(m)_Y ∘ l, r)  ~  (l, r ∘ R(m)_Y')
```

Identity (equation 2), representative `id_A`:

```text
⟨ θ_A(X) | θ'⁻¹_A(X') ⟩
```

Composition (equation 3). For `⟨l₁ | r₁⟩` on `f` and `⟨l₂ | r₂⟩` on `g`, the composite has representative `g ∘ f` and legs

```text
θ_{f,g}(Z) ∘ f*(l₂) ∘ l₁
r₁ ∘ f*'(r₂) ∘ θ'⁻¹_{f,g}(Z')
```

`optic::Witness` stores the representative. `optic::identity` and `optic::compose` are equations (2) and (3) for the slice indexed category. `optic::related_by` is one step of the generating relation; `optic::equivalent` searches 2-cells in either direction. Where the paper computes the coend, equality of normal forms is the decision procedure (`dlens::normalize`, `dprism::normalize`).

`θ` is the coherence of the indexed category: `θ_A: Id ⇒ id*` and `θ_{f,g}: f* ∘ g* ⇒ (g ∘ f)*`. Both are stored as the canonical comparisons of pullbacks (slices) or pushouts (coslices), not as existence proofs.

## Finite sets

`finset` is the skeletal category of finite sets. An object is a cardinality. A morphism `n → m` is a table of length `n` with values in `0..m`. The empty function exists out of the empty set, and out of a nonempty set only when the codomain is nonempty.

Product index of `(i, j)` in `A × B` is `i * |B| + j` (`pair_index`). Coproduct lays the left summand in `0..|A|` and the right summand after it.

Limits and colimits are the canonical cones, not an arbitrary isomorphic copy: terminal `1`, initial `0`, binary product and coproduct, pullback as the ordered subset of the product, pushout as the coequalizer of the two maps into the coproduct, equalizer, and coequalizer via union-find with roots numbered in order of first appearance. Mediators are `pair`, `copair`, `pullback_mediator`, `equalizer_mediator`, `coequalizer_mediator`, and `pushout_mediator`. `distribute_product_coproduct` is the distributivity isomorphism `A × (B ⊔ C) → (A × B) ⊔ (A × C)`.

`C/(A ⊔ B) ≅ C/A × C/B` is `slice::lextensive_split`: it returns the two pullbacks along the coproduct injections and the isomorphism of the total space. That is the lextensivity used for coproducts of lenses.

## Spans and cospans

`span::Span` is `left ← apex → right`. Composition is the pullback of the adjacent legs. The identity span on `A` is `A ←id— A —id→ A`. A 2-cell is a map of apices commuting with both legs (`two_cells`, `cell_commutes`). `right_unitor`, `left_unitor`, and `associator` are the canonical bijections; `associator(h, g, f)` goes from `(h ∘ g) ∘ f` to `h ∘ (g ∘ f)`.

`include_morphism` sends a function `f: A → B` to the span `A ←id— A —f→ B`. Lemma 1 is the test that `Span(A ⊔ B, C)` decomposes as a product of spans, via distributivity and lextensivity.

`span::Cospan` is the opposite bicategory: feet map into the apex, composition is a pushout. `coidentity`, `cocompose`, `co_two_cells`, `co_right_unitor`, `co_left_unitor`, and `co_associator` are the duals. `Cospan = Span^op`, as in Section 3.3.

## Indexed categories

`slice::SliceObj` is a morphism `total → base`. `SliceMor` is a map of totals commuting with the legs. `reindex_obj` along a span `A ← M → B` sends `Y → B` to the pullback `M ×_B Y`, structured over `A` by `M → A`. `reindex_mor` is the induced map of pullbacks. `theta_id` / `theta_id_inv` and `theta_comp` / `theta_comp_inv` are `θ`. `act_two_cell` is `L(m): f* Y → g* Y` for `m: f ⇒ g`, covariant in the 2-cell, which is the direction in the equivalence under Definition 1.

The pseudofunctor laws that the proof of Theorem 1 uses are tested directly:

```text
L(ρ_f) ∘ θ_{id, f} ∘ θ_A(f* Y) = id
L(λ_f) ∘ θ_{f, id} ∘ f*(θ_B(Y)) = id
L(α) ∘ θ_{g∘f, h} ∘ θ_{f, g}(h* Z) = θ_{f, h∘g} ∘ f*(θ_{g, h})
```

`slice::CosliceObj` is a morphism `base → total`. Reindexing along a cospan is a pushout. `co_theta_id`, `co_theta_comp`, `reindex_coslice`, and `co_act_two_cell` are the dual data, with the same identity and associativity checks.

## Dependent lenses

Objects of `dlens` are cospans `X → A ← X'`, stored as `optic::Family` (two slice objects with the same base). This is Definition 2: `DLens = Optic_{C/–, C/–}`.

Equation (4) is a witness: a span `A ← M → B` together with slice maps `X → M ×_B Y` and `M ×_B Y' → X'` over `A`. `dlens::embed` builds that witness from the normal form. The apex is the total space of `X`, the legs are `X → A` and `X → B` via `get`, and the forward leg is `x ↦ (x, get(x))`.

Equation (5) is `DLens`:

```text
⨿_{get: X → Y}  C/A(X ×_B Y', X')
```

The index runs over maps of total spaces. For each `get`, `X` is viewed as an object of `C/(A × B)` by `x ↦ (p_X(x), p_Y(get(x)))`, and the put is a slice map into `X'` out of the pullback of that object with `Y' → B`. `dlens::normalize` is the Yoneda step: `get` is the `Y`-component of the forward leg, and the put is the `M`-component pulled back along `Y'` and then composed with the backward leg. `normalize ∘ embed = id`, and `embed ∘ normalize` is related to the original witness by `normalizing_cell`.

Identity: `get = id_X` and `put = π₂: X ×_A X' → X'`.

Composition:

```text
get = get₂ ∘ get₁
put(x, z') = put₁(x, put₂(get₁(x), z'))
```

This agrees with equation (3) after normalizing the composite of the embedded witnesses. Units and associativity hold on the nose for normal forms.

Coproducts (Proposition 3, specialized as Proposition 4). The coproduct of cospans `(X_i → A_i ← X'_i)` is the cospan of coproducts. `injection` is the lens whose `get` is the forward coproduct injection and whose `put` recovers the summand. `copair` / `decompose` are the universal property: a lens out of the coproduct is exactly a family of lenses out of the summands. The empty coproduct is the empty cospan over the empty set, and there is exactly one lens out of it. The base is lextensive, which is why the fibres of a coproduct are the product of the fibres.

## Dependent prisms

`dprism` is Section 3.3. Objects are spans `X ← A → X'`, stored as `CoFamily` (two coslice objects). A witness is `PrismWitness`: a cospan representative and a pair of coslice maps, equation (8). `witness_id` and `witness_compose` are equations (2) and (3) with the coslice coherences.

The normal form is

```text
⨿_{Y' → X'}  A/C(X, X' ⊔_B Y)
```

indexed by maps of total spaces `Y' → X'`. `DPrism` stores that map as `back` and the coslice map `X → X' ⊔_B Y` as `fwd`. The pushout is taken in the same element order as reindexing along the embedded cospan whose apex is `X'` and whose right leg is `back` composed with `B → Y'`. `normalize ∘ embed = id`. Composition of normal forms is `normalize(witness_compose(embed(_), embed(_)))`, so it is equation (3).

The empty coproduct is not the empty-empty span. Over the initial base the forward fibre is initial (`0 → 0`) and the backward fibre is terminal (`0 → 1`), which is the unique object with exactly one prism out. A nonempty coproduct is componentwise on bases and totals, with legs injected into the corresponding summand.

## Functor lenses and mixed optics

Proposition 2. `functor_lens::object` is the family whose forward leg is `id_A` and whose backward leg is the given slice. A functor lens is a dependent lens between those families: `get: A → B` is the base map and the put is `A ×_B Y' → X'` over `A`. `is_functor_lens` recognizes them. Composition of functor lenses composes the base maps. `from_base` builds one from an explicit base map and put.

Proposition 1. `mixed` deloops a monoidal category to a one-object bicategory.

* `(FinSet, ×)` acting on itself is a classical lens: `get: X → Y` and `put: X × Y' → X'`. `lens_compose` is `get₂ ∘ get₁` and `put(x, z') = put₁(x, put₂(get₁(x), z'))`. `embed_lens` uses residual `X` and forward leg `x ↦ (x, get(x))`. `normalize_lens` is the Yoneda reduction. Identity is `(id, π₂)`.
* `(FinSet, ⊔)` is a classical prism: `build: Y' → X'` and `match: X → X' ⊔ Y`. Identity is `(id, inr)`. Composition folds the intermediate summand through `build` of the inner prism.

`Pair` is the pair of cardinalities `(forward, backward)`. There is no separate base object: the delooping has one.

## Monoidal lenses and prisms

The base symmetric monoidal category is `(FinSet, ×, 1)`. Product preserves equalizers and coequalizers; both are tested.

`monoid::CMon` is a carrier, a unit, and a row-major multiplication table (`i * carrier + j`). `monoids_up_to` enumerates the commutative monoids. `Module` is a carrier and an action table (`s * carrier + x`). `tensor` is the coequalizer

```text
X ⊗_B Y  =  coeq( X × B × Y  ⇉  X × Y )
```

of the two actions. The projection `X × Y → X ⊗_B Y` is returned with the cardinality of the quotient. `is_monoid_hom` and `is_module_hom` are the evident equations. The regular module is the monoid acting on itself; `action_descends` checks that this action coequalizes the tensor relation.

`comonoid` is the cartesian dual. Every object has exactly one commutative comonoid structure, the diagonal `i ↦ (i, i)`, and every function preserves it (`preserves_diagonal` compares `(f × f) ∘ δ` with `δ ∘ f`). A comodule is a map into the base. `comonoid::tensor` is the pullback of the two legs, which is the equalizer of the two coactions into `M × B × N`. Coproduct injections are comonoid homomorphisms, and comodules over `A ⊔ B` split as a pair of comodules (`lextensive_split`). That is the hypothesis of Proposition 5 on this base.

Proposition 5’s last sentence says `DLens_C`. The proof concludes `DLens_{C,⊗}`. The crate follows the proof. On `(FinSet, ×)` the two categories have the same normal forms: `monoidal_optic::as_comodule_put` is the pullback `X ×_B Y'`, and its underlying carrier is the domain of the dependent-lens put. The put’s leg over `A` is `p_X ∘ π`, not the leg of that pullback over `B`.

A monoidal prism, Section 3.3, is indexed by monoid homomorphisms. The paper writes the hom-set as `A/C(X, X' ⊗_B Y)`. The maps the coend produces are module homomorphisms, the dual of `Comod_A(X ⊗_B Y', X')`. `monoidal_optic::PrismData` is one summand: `back: B → A` a monoid homomorphism, and `into_tensor: X → X' ⊗_B Y` where `X'` is the regular `A`-module restricted along `back`. `prism_data` enumerates them. The identity monoid homomorphism appears whenever the two monoids are equal.

## Closed dependent optics

Section 3.4. The optic is closed when `(–)*' Y': B(A, B) → R_A` has a right adjoint `Y' ⊲ –`. Then

```text
∫^f L_A(X, f* Y) × R_A(f*' Y', X')  ≃  L_A(X, (Y' ⊲ X')* Y)
```

For slices, `M ↦ M ×_B Y'` is left adjoint to the dependent function space. The fibre of `Y' ⊲ X'` over `(a, b) ∈ A × B` is the set of functions from the fibre `Y'_b` to the fibre `X'_a`. One empty function is recorded when the domain fibre is empty. `closed::from_lens` packages a dependent lens’s `get` and `put` as the slice map `X → (Y' ⊲ X') ×_B Y` over `A`. `to_lens` is the inverse. `counit` is evaluation `(Y' ⊲ X') ×_B Y' → X'`.

## Tambara representations

Section 4, instantiated on dependent lenses and on classical lenses.

`tambara::iota(l, r)` is Definition 5. For `l: X₀ → X₁` and `r: X₁' → X₀'` over the same base,

```text
ι(l, r) = ⟨ θ ∘ l | r ∘ θ⁻¹ ⟩
```

on the identity span. Lemma 2: precomposing a lens with `ι(l, r)` replaces `get` by `get ∘ l` and `put` by `r ∘ put`. Lemma 3: postcomposing replaces `get` by `l ∘ get` and feeds `r` into the put. Proposition 6: `ι` preserves composition,

```text
ι(l₂, r₂) ∘ ι(l₁, r₁) = ι(l₂ ∘ l₁, r₁ ∘ r₂)
```

The composite backward map is `r₁ ∘ r₂`, which is the order produced by equation (3). Theorem 3 on the finite subcategory of families of cardinality at most 1: two lenses are equal if and only if they induce the same precomposition maps `DLens(cod, T) → DLens(dom, T)` for every target family `T`.

`tambara::strengthen(lens, c)` is the cartesian strength `id_C × lens` on a classical lens. The backward copy of `C` is the one that is kept: `put'((c, x), (c', y')) = (c', put(x, y'))`. It preserves identities and composition, and it satisfies the put-get law whenever both dimensions are positive.

## Bicategory of dependent optics

Remark 3. The coend quotient forgets the representative, so there is no pseudofunctor `Optic → B` on the nose. The bicategory keeps witnesses as 1-cells. A 2-cell is a 2-cell of spans that satisfies `optic::related_by`. `dlens::normalizing_cell` is such a 2-cell from the embedded normal form to the original witness, and `optic::equivalent` is the relation whose quotient is equality of normal forms. That quotient is `π₀`.

`bicat_optic::paste` is horizontal composition of 2-cells: the apex map of the composite span is the componentwise action of the two 2-cells on the pullback. When the pasted apex has the same enumeration as `span::compose`, the paste commutes with the legs. Composites of equivalent witnesses have the same normal form, which is the composite lens.

The pullback presentation (fibre optics §4.1) for the cartesian delooping is `mixed::LensWitness`: one residual and a pair of legs. `normalize_lens ∘ embed_lens = id`.

## Fibre optics

`fibre` is the matrix bicategory used in Section 5 to exhibit fibre optics as dependent optics. A 1-cell `I → J` is an `I × J` matrix of cardinalities (`Matrix`, row-major). Composition sums products of entries:

```text
(MN)_{ik} = Σ_j |M_{ij}| · |N_{jk}|
```

The identity matrix is `1` on the diagonal and `0` off it. Reindexing a family is matrix–vector multiplication `(M • Y)_i = Σ_j |M_{ij}| · |Y_j|`. The identity matrix acts as the identity, composition of matrices matches composition of actions, and a `1 × 1` matrix `(m)` acting on a one-part family `(y)` is the cartesian residual of cardinality `m · y`. `matrices(rows, cols, max_entry)` enumerates entries in `0..=max_entry`.

## Polynomial optics

`polynomial` is Milewski, *Compound Optics* §9, which the paper identifies with dependent optics. A `Poly` is a list of positions, each a pair `(coefficient cardinality, direction cardinality)`: `Σ_n s_n × y^{t_n}`.

A `PolyMap` sends each source coefficient to a `Choice`: a target position and a function `b_target → t_source` from the target directions to the source directions. Positions with coefficient cardinality `0` are not legal targets. Identity sends each position to itself with `id` on directions. Composition substitutes the choices and composes the direction maps (`outer` applied first). Coefficient sets in the tested range have size at most 1, so substitution uses the unique element of the intermediate position.

`residual` is the 0-1 matrix of positions: entry `(n, k)` is 1 when the (first) coefficient of source position `k` lands on target position `n`. Composition of polynomial maps multiplies these matrices in `fibre`. `polys(max_positions, max_card)` and `hom` enumerate the maps.

## Monoidal structure

Section 5. The slice indexed category is lax monoidal via the cartesian product. `monoidal_structure::tensor_family` sends `(X → A, Y → B)` to `X × Y → A × B`, and the same on the backward legs. `tensor_lens` is

```text
get = get₁ × get₂
put = put₁ × put₂
```

after splitting `(X × Y) ×_{A×B} (X' × Y')` into `(X ×_A X') × (Y ×_B Y')`. Tensor with the identity lens on a family is functorial: `(g ∘ f) ⊗ id = (g ⊗ id) ∘ (f ⊗ id)`, including the puts. The monoidal unit is the identity family on the terminal set `1 → 1`. Tensor on the left with that unit reproduces the original `get`, because `|1 × X| = |X|` and the product encoding puts the unique element of `1` in the high part of the index.

Functor lenses inherit this tensor (the monoidal Grothendieck construction). The forward family of a tensor of identity-forward families is again the identity on the product base, and the backward total space is the product of the backward total spaces.

Coproduct-shaped tensors of classical prisms are checked on identities: the identity prism on `A ⊔ A` has `build = id`, and that map is the copair of the two identity builds.

## Reverse-mode differentiation on rationals

`ad` is the scalar case of Section 3.1. `Q` is a reduced rational (`i64` numerator and positive denominator). No division and no floating point.

`Expr` is variables (de Bruijn in `Let`), constants, negation, addition, multiplication, `let`, and `if_eq`. `forward` returns a value and a `Tape`. `backward` returns one cotangent per environment slot. `stored_scalars` lists the primal rationals the backward pass actually reads:

| Operation | What is stored |
|---|---|
| addition, negation, variable, constant, projection | nothing |
| multiplication | both factors, not the product |
| `if_eq` | only the branch that was taken |

Cotangents agree with forward-mode dual numbers `Q[ε]/ε²` on a fixed catalogue (a degree-2 polynomial, a `let` that duplicates a product, a projection, both branches of `if_eq`, and the primitives), at integer coordinates in `-2..=2`. The backward function depends on the tape and the cotangent, not on a second copy of the environment.

## Kernels

`kernel` is how an existing architecture becomes a dependent lens whose two passes are NVIDIA kernels. The memory those kernels run against is specified in [Compiler specification](#compiler-specification).

A stage is one lens `X → M × Y`:

| Stage | Forward | Residual `M` |
|---|---|---|
| `Gemm` | `Y = relu?(X W + b)` | the input `X` (`batch * din` floats). If `relu`, also one `u8` per output, `1` when the pre-activation is positive |
| `Attention` | self-attention with `Q = K = V = X` | the tokens (`batch * tokens * dim` floats) and the softmax rows (`batch * tokens * tokens` floats) |
| `Add` | `Y = Y + tape[from]` | nothing. `from` is the offset of an activation some earlier stage already saved |

`W` is stored input-major: `W[k * dout + n]` is the weight from input `k` to output `n`, then `b` of length `dout`. Parameters of later stages are concatenated in that order. The tape is append-only. `tape_f32` and `tape_u8` are its lengths.

Sequencing is optic composition: the composite residual is the concatenation of the stage residuals, and the backward pass walks the stages in reverse. A skip is the unit-residual add. Its cotangent is added onto the slot named by `from` when the stage that saved that slot runs backward. Nothing is stored twice.

The three constructors:

* `Program::mlp(batch, dims)` — linear layers, ReLU between them and not after the last. For `mlp(2, &[3, 4, 2])` the tape is `2*3 + 2*4` floats and `2*4` mask bytes, and the parameter buffer has length `3*4 + 4 + 4*2 + 2`.
* `Program::resnet_block(batch, dim, hidden)` — `x + W₂ relu(W₁ x + b₁) + b₂`. The first GEMM saves `x`. The add’s `from` is that offset.
* `Program::transformer_block(batch, tokens, dim, mlp_hidden)` — residual self-attention, then a residual MLP of width `mlp_hidden`. Attention saves the tokens; its skip reuses them. The MLP’s first GEMM saves the attention output; its skip reuses that. The softmax row is streamed, so the token count is whatever fits in a 64-bit length.

Attention, with `scale = 1/√dim`:

```text
S_ij = scale · ⟨X_i, X_j⟩
A = softmax(S)          (row-wise, stable via subtracting the max)
Y_i = Σ_j A_ij X_j
```

The backward map is the sum of the three paths `dQ + dK + dV`, with softmax backward `dS_ij = A_ij (dA_ij − Σ_k A_ik dA_ik)` and `dA_ij = ⟨dY_i, X_j⟩`.

`forward_backward(prog, x, params)` runs the lens for the sum loss (cotangent of the output is identically 1) and returns `(output, d_input, d_params)`. `init(n, scale)` is the deterministic positive vector `scale * ((i % 7) + 1) / 8`, used so ReLU stays off its kink during finite differences. Finite differences use step `1e-3` and accept absolute error below `2e-2`.

`emit_cuda` prints a standalone CUDA program. Each GEMM becomes `fwd_gemm`, `bwd_relu` (when needed), `bwd_dx`, `bwd_dw`, `bwd_db`, and `add_skip`. Attention becomes `fwd_attn` and `bwd_attn`. Adds become `add_fwd`, used both to apply the skip and to scatter its cotangent into the side buffer. Launches use 128 threads. The generated `main` copies the Rust inputs and the Rust lens outputs into the binary and checks the GPU results with tolerance `1e-3 + 1e-3 |expected|`.

`nvcc_run` writes the source under the system temp directory, compiles it, and runs it:

```text
/usr/local/cuda/bin/nvcc -arch=sm_75 -ccbin g++-11 -O2 <name>.cu -o <name>
```

`sm_75` is the compute capability of the GeForce RTX 2060 this crate was run on. CUDA 12.0’s `nvcc` does not accept GCC 13, so the host compiler is `g++-11`. The test `nvidia_kernels_match_the_lens` does this for the MLP `mlp(2, &[3, 4, 2])`, the ResNet block `resnet_block(2, 3, 4)`, and the transformer block `transformer_block(1, 3, 4, 5)`. A successful run prints `OK` and the program name.

To lower another architecture of the same three stages, build a `Program` whose stages obey the tape rules above (each `Gemm`/`Attention` saves exactly the tensors its backward kernel reads, and each `Add` names one of those saves). `emit_cuda` does not depend on which constructor produced the program. Mixtral adds norms, rotary embeddings, and a routed mixture, so it has its own emitter.

## Mixtral

`mixtral` is Mixtral 8x7B (Apache-2.0, Mistral AI, arXiv:2401.04088) as the lens of Section 3.1. The forward map writes a residual that contains only what the backward map reads. The backward map returns one cotangent per parameter. SGD subtracts a step along that cotangent. Routing, RMSNorm, and RoPE do not fit the three stages in `kernel`, so this module does not build a `kernel::Program`.

`MixtralConfig::mixtral_8x7b` is the published shape: vocabulary 32000, width 4096, 32 layers, 32 query heads, 8 key/value heads, head dimension 128, expert hidden size 14336, 8 experts, top 2, RMSNorm ε = `1e-5`, RoPE base `1e6`, sliding window 4096. `parameter_count` is the number of stored weights, about 46.7 billion. That configuration is counted and not allocated.

`MixtralConfig::demo` is the same block at vocabulary 32, width 32, 2 layers, 4 query heads, 2 key/value heads, head dimension 8, expert hidden size 48, 4 experts, top 2, window 4, and the same ε and RoPE base. `demo_batch` is 2 sequences of length 8. `parameter_count` of the demo is 45472. `emit_cuda` compiles this demo.

Parameters are concatenated, each matrix row-major with the contraction index first (`W[k * dout + n]`):

1. token embedding, `vocab * dim`
2. each layer, in order: attention RMSNorm (`dim`), `Wq` (`dim * heads * head_dim`), `Wk` and `Wv` (`dim * kv_heads * head_dim` each), `Wo` (`heads * head_dim * dim`), feed-forward RMSNorm (`dim`), router (`dim * experts`), then per expert `W_gate` and `W_up` (`dim * intermediate`) and `W_down` (`intermediate * dim`)
3. final RMSNorm (`dim`)
4. untied language-model head (`dim * vocab`)

There are no biases.

The residual stream `x` through one layer is

```text
x = x + Attn(RMSNorm(x))
x = x + MoE(RMSNorm(x))
```

RMSNorm is `y = x * rsqrt(mean(x²) + ε) * weight`. The backward pass sees the weighted output: the linear map after the norm reads `x * rsqrt * weight`, and the weight gradient is taken against that same vector.

RoPE is Hugging Face `rotate_half`. On each head the halves `(q1, q2)` become `(q1 cos − q2 sin, q2 cos + q1 sin)`, with `inv_freq_i = θ^(−2i / head_dim)` and angle `position * inv_freq`. Cosine and sine are shared by the two halves. The adjoint is the same rotation with the sign of `sin` flipped.

Grouped-query attention uses key/value head `query_head / (heads / kv_heads)` and scale `1/√head_dim`. A key is visible when `key ≤ query` and `query − key < window` (the window includes the current token). Softmax runs over the visible positions. `Wo` is bias-free.

The router computes `logits = x W` over every expert, softmaxes all of them, keeps the top `k` probabilities (higher probability, then the lower expert index), and divides those `k` values by their sum. The mixture is `Σ_k w_k · Expert_{i_k}(x)`. An expert is bias-free SwiGLU, `W_down(silu(x W_gate) ⊙ x W_up)`, with `silu(z) = z · sigmoid(z)`. The adjoint of a hard top-k choice flows only through the selected experts and through the softmax-plus-renormalization Jacobian of the kept probabilities. An expert that no token selected has a parameter gradient of zero.

The language-model head is bias-free on the final RMSNorm. The loss is the mean token cross-entropy over `batch * seq` positions. Its logit cotangent is `(softmax(logits) − one_hot(target)) / (batch * seq)`.

Per layer, the residual holds:

| Saved | Read by |
|---|---|
| stream activation entering attention, and its RMSNorm `rsqrt` | attention-norm backward, and the skip around attention |
| pre-RoPE queries and keys, and the values | RoPE adjoint and attention backward |
| attention probabilities, and the mixed heads | softmax backward and `Wo` |
| stream activation entering the feed-forward, and its RMSNorm `rsqrt` | feed-forward-norm backward, and the skip around the mixture |
| router probabilities over every expert, top-k indices, the probabilities before renormalization, the mixture weights | router Jacobian |
| gate and up projections | SwiGLU backward |

The CPU tape stores gate and up for the selected slots only. The CUDA tape stores them for every expert, and an unselected expert's outgoing cotangent is zero. Those two adjoints agree. Weights stay in the parameter buffer. The runner also keeps each layer's weighted norm outputs and the attention mixture, because a later norm overwrites the live activation. RoPE angles are recomputed from the pre-RoPE queries and keys. Attention scores are streamed, so the sequence length is a 64-bit count.

Backward order is the language-model head, the final RMSNorm, the layers from last to first, then a scatter-add into the embedding rows. Inside a layer: expert SwiGLU and the router, the feed-forward RMSNorm, the skip around the mixture, `Wo`, grouped-query attention, the RoPE adjoint, `Wq`/`Wk`/`Wv`, the attention RMSNorm, and the skip around attention. Both branches of a residual receive the cotangent of the sum. Token ids are data, not parameters.

`init_params` is `0.02 * ((i % 5) + 1)`. `init_router` then overwrites each router column with `0.05 * (e + 1) + 0.001 * d`, so the top two experts stay selected under a finite-difference step of `1e-3`. `demo_batch` uses token `(i * 5 + 1) mod vocab` and target `(token + 3) mod vocab`.

`forward_backward` returns the loss, the full parameter gradient, and the logits. `sgd_step` replaces `p` with `p − lr · ∇p` and returns the Euclidean norm of the gradient. The demo step uses `DEMO_LR` (`0.05`).

`emit_cuda(cfg, batch, seq, probes, cluster)` fills `src/staged_mixtral.cu.tpl` from `compile_mixtral`. The generated `STEPS` table is the stage plan: device, parameter home, pass, operation, parameter offset, and tile bounds. `kernel::nvcc_run` compiles and runs that source:

```text
/usr/local/cuda/bin/nvcc -arch=sm_75 -ccbin g++-11 -O2 mixtral_staged.cu -o mixtral_staged
```

A GPU step launches the kernels for that window and copies the tile from its home. A CPU step runs the same operation in the host interpreter. SGD is one step per parameter home, on the device that stores the home, and updates that rectangle with `p -= lr * dp`. The binary prints `PCOUNT`, `LOSS0`, `LOSS1`, `GSUM`, `GABS`, `GNORM`, `GPUOPS`, `CPUOPS`, `XFERS`, and one `GRAD` line per probe. `nvidia_mixtral_matches_the_adjoint` compares the loss, the gradient sum, the absolute sum, the Euclidean norm, and the probe gradients to the Rust lens, with tolerance `1e-3 + 1e-3 |expected|`, and checks that the loss went down. The test cluster is a 64 MiB CPU, which holds the parameter homes and applies SGD, and a 16 KiB GPU, which runs the tiled windows. `GPUOPS` and `XFERS` are both positive.

Finite differences of the demo, step `1e-3`, accept absolute error below `2e-2` on eight parameters: an embedding row, the first layer's attention norm, query, and output projection, a selected gate and down matrix, the final norm, and the language-model head.

## Compiler specification

A compiled model is a schedule. The schedule is code memory: kernel text plus an ordered list of launches. Every value a launch reads or writes is buffer memory. A launch is legal when each slice it names is resident on the device that runs it, that device's buffer can hold the resident bytes, and each kernel phase fits that device's code budget. Weights and their adjoint updates are slices of two buffers, so a stage can run while the rest of the model stays outside the working set. [`cluster`](src/cluster.rs) is the pass that places those slices on a heterogeneous cluster.

### Code memory

Code memory has two regions.

**Kernel text.** Device functions. The small compiler emits, per `Gemm` stage, `fwd_gemm`, `bwd_relu`, `bwd_dx`, `bwd_dw`, `bwd_db`, and `add_skip`, and per `Attention` stage `fwd_attn` and `bwd_attn`. An `Add` emits `add_fwd`. The Mixtral compiler emits one copy of each kind: `k_embed`, `k_embed_bwd`, `k_rmsnorm`, `k_rmsnorm_bwd`, `k_rmsnorm_dw`, `k_lin_y`, `k_lin_dx`, `k_lin_dw`, `k_add`, `k_zero`, `k_rope`, `k_gqa_fwd`, `k_gqa_bwd`, `k_router`, `k_silu_fwd`, `k_silu_bwd`, `k_mix_fwd`, `k_mix_bwd`, `k_loss`, `k_sgd`. The router Jacobian is `k_mix_bwd`. A block is 128 threads. The grid is `(n + 127) / 128` blocks. CUDA allows `2^31 - 1` blocks in one launch, so a longer buffer is issued as successive chunks of that many blocks. The thread index is `base + blockIdx.x * blockDim.x + threadIdx.x`, with `base` a 64-bit element offset.

**The schedule.** Host instructions. For Mixtral each entry is one staged window: the device, the parameter home, the pass, the operation, the parameter offset, and the batch, row, and column bounds. The layer and expert order is the order of those windows. Launches run one at a time on the default CUDA stream. Two launches may be reordered or overlapped only when no write slice of either meets a read or write slice of the other. Atomic updates of one slice (below) stay in the order written in the schedule.

Weights, activations, residuals, and adjoints are not kernel text. `mixtral::emit_cuda` fills `P` at runtime with `init_params` and `init_router`. `kernel::emit_cuda` additionally copies the parameter buffer and the expected Rust results into the translation unit so the binary can check itself; those arrays initialize buffers, and the kernels still receive them as pointers.

### Buffer memory

A buffer is an element type, a length in elements, and a base address. A slice is `(buffer, offset, length)` with `offset + length ≤ buffer.length`. A kernel pointer is `base + offset`. Lengths and offsets are `u64`. `mixtral::emit_cuda` accepts every positive shape, including `MixtralConfig::mixtral_8x7b` at about 46.7 billion parameters, and `nvcc_compile` compiles that schedule. The published binary is compiled and not executed. Executing it would allocate the host parameter and adjoint vectors; each GPU launch allocates the window's tile. The caller supplies the cluster budgets that choose those tiles.

| Buffer | Element | Length | Who writes it | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `P` | `f32` | parameter count, in the layout above | `k_sgd` after the adjoint is finished | forward kernels, and backward kernels that still need the weights (`bwd_dx`, `k_linear_dx`, RMSNorm backward, router backward) |
| `dP` | `f32` | the same layout as `P` | adjoint kernels, after a zeroing of the whole buffer | `k_sgd` |
| residual `f32` | `f32` | `tape_f32`, or the Mixtral residual below | the forward launch that saves it | the backward launch of that same stage |
| residual mask | `u8` | `tape_u8` | `fwd_gemm` when ReLU is on | `bwd_relu` |
| side | `f32` | `tape_f32`, zeroed before backward | backward of an `Add`, at offset `from` | backward of the stage that saved that offset, added into `dX` |
| activation pair | `f32` | the longest activation in the program | the stage that produces it | the next stage. The two buffers alternate |
| tokens, targets | `i32` | `batch * seq` | the caller | embedding and the loss |
| loss | `f32` | 1 | `k_ce`, by atomic add | the host |

`P` and `dP` use the same offsets. A GEMM or Mixtral linear map occupies `din * dout` floats at its offset, then a bias of `dout` floats when the stage has one. Mixtral has no biases. An assigning writer (`bwd_dw`, `bwd_db`, `k_linear_dw`) is the only writer of its slice and stores the full gradient. An atomic writer is the only writer of its slice, runs after the zeroing, and adds. The atomic writers are the embedding rows, an RMSNorm weight, the router, and the grouped-query `dk` and `dv` across heads that share a key/value head.

### Small-program schedule

Forward follows `Program.stages`. Backward is that list reversed. Before backward, `dP` and the side buffer are zero, and the output cotangent is 1 in every coordinate (the sum loss).

| Launch | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|
| GEMM forward | activation, `P` weights, `P` bias | residual copy of the activation; ReLU mask when enabled; next activation |
| Attention forward | activation | residual copy of the tokens; residual softmax rows; next activation |
| Add forward | residual at `from` | current activation, by adding the residual into it |
| GEMM backward | output cotangent, residual activation, ReLU mask, `P` weights, side at the save | `dP` weights, `dP` bias, next cotangent (weight adjoint plus the side buffer) |
| Attention backward | output cotangent, residual tokens, residual probabilities, side at the token save | next cotangent |
| Add backward | output cotangent | side buffer at `from`, by adding the cotangent into it |

The reversed order puts each Add's backward before the backward of the stage that owns `from`, so the side slot is full when that stage adds it into `dX`.

### Mixtral schedule

Forward is embedding, then each layer, then the final norm and the language-model head, then `k_ce`. Backward is the head, the final norm, the layers from last to first, the embedding scatter, then `k_sgd`. Inside a layer the forward order is attention norm, `Wq`/`Wk`/`Wv`, RoPE, grouped-query attention, `Wo`, the attention residual, feed-forward norm, router, every expert's gate, up, SwiGLU, and down, then the mixture residual. The backward order is the experts and the router, the feed-forward norm, the mixture skip, `Wo`, grouped-query attention, the RoPE adjoint, `Wq`/`Wk`/`Wv`, the attention norm, and the attention skip.

Residual slots for one layer, with `N = batch * seq`. The CUDA schedule stores gate and up for every expert. The CPU tape stores them for the selected slots only (`N * top_k * intermediate` instead of `experts * N * intermediate`).

| Slot | Elements |
|---|---|
| stream into attention, stream into the feed-forward | `N * dim` each |
| attention and feed-forward `rsqrt` | `N` each |
| pre-RoPE queries, attention mixture | `N * heads * head_dim` each |
| pre-RoPE keys, values | `N * kv_heads * head_dim` each |
| attention probabilities | `batch * heads * seq * seq` |
| router probabilities | `N * experts` |
| top-k indices (`u64`), pre-renorm probabilities, mixture weights | `N * top_k` each |
| gate and up, all experts | `experts * N * intermediate` each |

The final norm adds one more stream of `N * dim` and one `rsqrt` of `N`. Across `L` layers the residual is `L` copies of the table.

Scratch is reused across layers. These aliases are part of the schedule; a reordering has to keep the same live ranges.

| Scratch | Holds | Until |
|---|---|---|
| stream `x` | layer input, then the layer cotangent | the attention-norm backward overwrites it with the incoming cotangent |
| `xn` | weighted feed-forward norm | expert and router adjoints have read it; attention backward then reuses it for the weighted attention norm |
| expert outputs | one vector per expert per token | the router adjoint has dotted them with the layer cotangent |
| `attn` | feed-forward-norm adjoint plus the mixture skip | the attention skip has added it into the incoming cotangent |
| `d_xn` | sum of expert and router input adjoints, later the sum of the `Wq`/`Wk`/`Wv` input adjoints | the matching RMSNorm backward reads it |
| pre-RoPE copies | queries, keys, values | RoPE has rebuilt the forward values and the attention adjoint has consumed them |

One layer's launches name that layer's slices of `P` and `dP`, that layer's residual slots, and the scratch. The other layers' weights are not in the working set. One published layer is this many floats of `P`:

```text
dim
+ dim * (heads * head_dim)
+ 2 * dim * (kv_heads * head_dim)
+ (heads * head_dim) * dim
+ dim
+ dim * experts
+ experts * (2 * dim * intermediate + intermediate * dim)
```

For the published shape that is 1,451,270,144 floats, about 5.41 GiB. The staged plan keeps that layer's parameters at their homes and moves one tile at a time. The adjoint of a tile is added into `dP` while the tile is resident.

`k_sgd` is the last group of windows, one per parameter home. It reads that home's `dP` rectangle and writes `P[i] ← P[i] − lr · dP[i]` on the device that stores the home. The demo passes `lr = 0.05`.

### Cluster staging

A cluster is a list of devices. Each device is a CPU or a GPU, with `buffer_bytes` for parameters, tapes, and scratch, and `code_bytes` for the kernels resident at one time. `compile_mixtral` and `compile_program` turn a model into a `StagePlan`.

Homes come first. Parameter rectangles are packed into buffer memory, CPUs first, splitting a matrix by rows and then by columns until each piece fits a device. The plan covers every parameter element once. A cluster whose buffers sum to less than the parameter bytes cannot hold the model; that is the capacity check, and it is independent of the model shape.

Tapes are homes too. Every Mixtral layer tape (the residual in the table above, including every expert's gate and up projections and the attention probabilities) is reserved while the windows are placed, then released, so the returned homes are the parameters. Peak buffer use during placement includes those tapes. A `Program` keeps its whole tape resident, because a skip reads an earlier save.

A window is one tiled launch. Its resident bytes are the homes already on that device, the parameter tile, the tape slice the tile reads, the activations, and the adjoint tile. The adjoint is applied while the tile is resident, so the schedule does not have to allocate a second full copy of `P`.

The compiler derives the fabric with `derive_links`. CPUs are connected to each other. Each GPU is attached to one CPU, and GPUs attached to the same CPU are connected to each other. With no CPU, the GPUs form a peer fabric. `Cluster::linked` replaces that fabric with an explicit one. `route_message` then chooses the shortest path for each message. An intermediate device is usable when its free buffer holds the message. A direct link has no intermediate, so the endpoints' own homes do not have to leave extra room.

Those routes become the message schedule. Before a window, inbound hops deliver the parameter rectangle and the activation tensor to the device that will run it. After a backward window, outbound hops return the adjoint rectangle to the parameter home and the activation tensor to the device that keeps it between windows. SGD runs at the home when that device can load `k_sgd`. When it cannot, the rectangle and its adjoint move to a device that can, the update runs there, and the updated rectangle comes back. The emitted program performs each hop with a copy along that link, then runs the window. Forward, adjoint, and update therefore stay in one sequence, and the only extra requirement is that every tile fits a device and every message has a route.

The tile shrinks the batch, the contraction, and the output until some device can hold the footprint and can load the kernels. A GPU loads CUDA kernels in phases: a phase is a subset whose code sizes sum to at most `code_bytes`. Each kernel is `KERNEL_CODE_BYTES` (8 KiB) in this model. A CPU runs the interpreter when its code budget covers `CPU_RUNTIME_BYTES` (64 KiB). GPU windows are preferred when both kinds fit. A GPU whose code budget holds only a few kernels still runs the tile, one phase at a time, with the buffers left in place.

`compile_mixtral(&MixtralConfig::mixtral_8x7b(), 1, 1, cluster)` is the published model on a chosen cluster. On a host with 256 GiB of buffer and a GPU with 6 GiB of buffer and 8 KiB of code, the parameter homes sit on the CPU and the GPU computes the tiles, loading kernels in more than one phase. `emit_cuda` on that cluster is what `published_mixtral_compiles_at_full_parameter_count` compiles. `emit_cuda` runs every window in that order, with the routed hops before and after it. GPU windows launch their kernels, and CPU windows run the host interpreter. The demo adjoint test uses a 64 MiB CPU for the homes and SGD and a 16 KiB GPU for the tiled compute. Two GPUs that each hold about half of the demo split the parameter homes, and each home's SGD window runs on the GPU that stores it.

## Vector spaces over GF(2)

`vect::Lin` is a linear map of dimension at most a few. Column `j` is the bitset of the image of basis vector `j`. The zero space has one map, the zero map, including maps into or out of it. `compose`, `add` (xor of columns), `transpose`, and `kronecker` are the structure maps. Kronecker order is `a * dim_b + b`. `all(dom, cod)` lists all `2^{dom·cod}` maps. Identities, the axiom `f + f = 0`, and `id ⊗ id = id` are tested up to dimension 2.

`bimod::closed_dimension(dx, dy, dxp, dyp)` is `dx · dy' · dx' · dy`, the dimension of `Hom(X, [Y', X'] ⊗ Y)` in Section 3.4 for the delooping of `(FinVect_{F₂}, ⊗)`. `vect::all(dx, that dimension)` has `2` to that power elements. For dimensions `(1, 1, 1, 1)` the set-level coend over residuals of dimension at most 1 is nonempty; that count is not the number of points of the vector-space coend, because the coend in `FinVect` uses the tensor product of hom-spaces rather than their cartesian product.

`enriched::closed_hom` lists those linear maps, as the points of the enriched hom-object (Remark 1, Clarke et al. Definition 2.1 specialized to this closed form). `compose_closed` is composition of linear maps. On dimension 0 and 1 it is associative and bilinear: `h ∘ (f + g) = h ∘ f + h ∘ g`.
